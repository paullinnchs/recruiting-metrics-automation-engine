# Demo Talk Track

A natural ~2–3 minute answer to: *"Paul, explain how this actually works."*

Not a scripted pitch — read it once, then say it in your own words.

---

Here's the simplest way to think about it. Most companies run two separate conversations that never actually talk to each other: one about how recruiting is performing, and one about whether workforce billing is actually correct. Different teams, different spreadsheets, different review cycles. This closes that gap.

*[Show the intake folder]*

It starts with one intake — the client sends us their recruiting data and their billing data together, as one package. This is stuff they already have sitting in their ATS and their finance systems — we're not asking them to build anything new.

*[Run the command]*

Then we run it through the engine.

*[Let it execute]*

First thing it does is check what it actually got — which parts of the data are complete enough to analyze, and which aren't. That matters, because if something's missing, I want to know that upfront, not find out three pages into a report that a number's wrong.

Assuming the data's there, it does two things in parallel. It runs a recruiting-performance analysis — time to fill, time to hire, offer acceptance, how new hires are actually performing once they're in the door. And separately, it runs what we call the Workforce Revenue Leak Workflow Sprint — that's the piece that looks at billing, rate cards, and contracts, and flags where money's actually leaking. Rates that don't match what was agreed. Billing that kept going after a rate card expired. Spend going through suppliers that were never approved.

*[Open the report]*

And then — this is the part that actually matters — it doesn't hand back two separate documents. It combines both into one report. One executive summary. One ranked list of what needs attention first, whether that's a hiring bottleneck or a six-figure billing issue. One set of next steps.

For this sample client, it flagged about $48,000 in revenue exposure, plus a handful of recruiting metrics that were worth a second look — and all of it's sitting in one document, generated in the same run.

That's the whole idea: one intake, one analysis, one report — instead of two teams doing this work separately and someone having to stitch it together by hand afterward.
